Context
Designed for India. Built for scale.
Indian SMBs often struggle to adapt global e-commerce platforms like Shopify. As sole product designer, I led end-to-end design of a web-based admin from scratch — removing app dependencies and enabling local compliance (GST, UPI).

The Problem
What was broken
Shopify was never built for India.
Existing platforms created compounding costs — transaction fees, app dependencies, and premium tier lock-ins. Merchants couldn't adapt checkout flows, implement local compliance, or track behaviour without expensive add-ons.
Pain Points
Hidden costs from transaction fees and app dependencies for basic features
Rigid checkout flows — no customisation without Shopify Plus
No local optimisation — GST, UPI, and regional behaviour absent by default
Overwhelming setup for non-tech-savvy merchants
No customer behaviour data (wishlist, cart drops) without costly plugins
Design Goals
Build an affordable, scalable admin for the Indian SMB ecosystem
Create intuitive self-serve UX — reduce developer reliance
Empower merchants to track customer behaviour and optimise accordingly
Offer deep insights without expensive third-party integrations
Who We Designed For

Feature Architecture
01 — Product Module
Product Module
Inventory to pricing including GST and HSN, variant control, and product tracking — all localised for Indian commerce.
02 — Orders
Order Management
View, create, return, cancel, and exchange orders with dual control — merchant and user side — with logic-based status flows.
03 — Collections
Collection Management
Flexible, customisable collections — not locked behind Liquid code. All products live within collections unless marked standalone.
04 — Customers
Customer Management
Deep analytics — order history, wishlist, carts, and behaviour-based insights for targeted marketing. All native, no add-ons.
05 — Settings
Setup & Settings
Configure refunds, payment modes, return durations, and platform behaviour — flexible, not locked to a pricing tier.
06 — Compliance
Extras & Compliance
Review resolution loop, integrated UPI checkout, and GST-first architecture — India-specific from the ground up.

Design Process
How I worked
01
Discovery
Stakeholder interviews from past Shopify setups. Competitive audit across Shopify, WooCommerce, Amazon Seller Central, Flipkart Seller Hub — focusing on module depth and India-specific gaps.
02
Synthesis & IA
Mapped high-friction areas in checkout, returns, and product upload. Modular IA built for future scalability — marketing automation and multi-store support as future slots.
03
Wireframing & Validation
Used Atlassian Design System for rapid, scalable design. Internal reviews with devs and business stakeholders. Key decision: separate analytics tabs from action tabs throughout.
04
Design Execution
Clean, minimal UIs across all 6 modules. Reusable component library for faster iteration. A design system devs could extend without returning to design for every edge case.
Constraints I worked within
No direct user testing — validated with internal stakeholders only
Limited engineering bandwidth — prioritised MVP flow first
Shifting business needs — adapted mid-project to prioritise GST/UPI
All features had to be natively built — no plugin shortcuts

Reflections
Key Learning
I assumed merchants wanted full control. Turns out they mostly wanted clarity and smart defaults. The most valuable design decision was choosing sensible defaults — not maximum flexibility.
What I'd do differently
Validate early prototypes with real merchants — even with mock data. Internal stakeholder reviews are valuable, but watching an actual merchant attempt to create their first product is irreplaceable.
What didn't work
Multi-store support was cut due to dev bandwidth. The early order module was too detailed — streamlined to show only essential actions. Wishlist marketing automation deprioritised post-MVP.
What this built in me
Building from scratch forces ruthless clarity about core workflows. This project sharpened the ability to separate what's essential from what's decorative — a skill that applies to every product problem since.
