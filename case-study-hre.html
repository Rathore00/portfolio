Context
$107M saved. Zero dashboards. Until now.
HRE (Hotel Rate Efficiency) is an automated engine that re-books hotel reservations when a lower rate becomes available. It had generated $107M+ in savings with no analytics surface — leadership was flying blind.

The Problem
What was invisible
A system generating millions with no one watching.
HRE auto-rebook volume grew 64% year-over-year (307K → 504K segments). SABRE supplier source grew from 5% to 43% share. Rejection volumes spiked to 521K in Jan 2026. Leadership saw none of it.
Leadership pain points
No single place to monitor system performance — every question required a manual SQL pull
No shared vocabulary for what "system success" meant across the team
36,252 total booking failures with zero visibility or root-cause path
SABRE structural shift not visible despite data existing in tables

The Design
Tab 01
Executive Summary
KPI scorecard strip answering the headline number, followed by charts explaining it. Entry point for all senior stakeholders.
Tab 02
Rebook Trends
Auto-rebook volume over time, savings trend, rejection spike patterns — all with full timeline visibility to surface the Jan 2026 event.
Tab 03
Clients & Chains
Top clients by savings volume, hotel chain performance, geographic savings distribution (USA = 83% of total).
Tab 04
GDS & Supplier Source
100% stacked area chart showing SABRE's 5%→43% share growth across 2022–2026. The structural story that single-period charts cannot tell.
Tab 05
Booking Failures
36K+ failures categorised by type, trend, and resolution status. Drill-down from high-level signal to root cause — no SQL required.
Tab 06
Rejection Analysis
521K rejection spike in Jan 2026 visualised against full historical baseline. Pattern recognition built into the layout.

Key Decisions
Rejected: spec-faithful tab structure
The KPI spec grouped 34 KPIs into 7 data-topology categories — defined by what tables they came from. I restructured around intent-based questions instead. Leaders don't open a dashboard wondering "what are my GDS metrics?"
Rejected: Sankey diagrams for rate flow
61.7% of booked source was labelled "UNKNOWN" and 97% of segments stayed on the same source. Sankey was visually incoherent. Replaced with a diverging bar chart that directly encodes the directional question.
Rejected: donut chart for rate types
10 rate types with an 84% savings variance. The insight — highest-volume ≠ highest-value — is invisible in a donut. A bubble chart encodes volume, savings-per-segment, and rate type identity simultaneously.
Insight from this project
Data analysis as discovery was unexpectedly powerful. The SABRE growth story, rejection spike, and USA savings concentration all emerged from time in the data — none were in the brief. For projects without a research function, this is the honest starting point.

What I'd do differently
Run 2–3 stakeholder interviews before design — not relying on spec alone
Prioritise 10 KPIs rigorously before designing all 34
Plan a structured post-launch review 3–4 weeks after go-live
