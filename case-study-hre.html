Hotel Rate Efficiency BI Dashboard

HRE had been quietly re-booking hotel reservations at lower rates for years. The engine worked. The problem was that no one could see how — or where.

Context

Millions of $ saved. Zero visibility. Until now.

HRE (Hotel Rate Efficiency) is an automated engine that re-books hotel reservations when a lower rate becomes available. It had generated $107M+ in savings with no analytics surface, leadership was flying blind.

  • Stakeholders managed a growing operation through spreadsheets and a non-functional Power BI dashboard 01
  • No shared view of system performance, rejection trends, or savings distribution 02
  • Informal monitoring no longer viable at this scale 03
Problems & Constraints

Too much data. No shared understanding of what to act on.

The issue wasn’t a lack of data — it was an absence of structure around it. Each team interpreted the numbers differently, and there was no shared frame of reference for what required attention.

Leadership pain points

Underlying data partially raw and uncategorised — not ready to visualise uniformly

Leadership pain points

Leadership pain points

Leadership pain points

Research & Discovery

Starting with the data,
because the brief couldn’t

With no research function on the project, discovery started with systematic data analysis. Three patterns emerged that hadn’t been articulated in any brief — and each shaped the information architecture that followed.

01 Repeated stakeholder sessions identified not just what data existed, but what decisions stakeholders were trying to make with it
02 Domain learning was a necessary investment — hotel reshop terminology, supplier dynamics, and operational logic all informed design decisions
03 The ramp-up extended the timeline but grounded every subsequent call in context rather than assumption
Finding 01
Growth story

Reshop volume was growing at a rate the existing tooling couldn’t track or communicate.

Finding 02
Rejection spikes

Periodic rejection spikes correlated with specific supplier behaviour — invisible in the previous dashboard.

Finding 03
Savings concentration

A narrow band of bookings accounted for a disproportionate share of savings — relevant for prioritisation.

Research & Discovery

Data analysis as discovery was unexpectedly powerful. The growth story, rejection spike, and savings concentration all emerged from time in the data — none were in the brief. For projects without a research function, this is the honest starting point.

GDS Insight
Design Process & Collaboration

Structuring volume without overwhelming the people who need to act on it

Earlier explorations used a single scrollable view with progressive disclosure. Consistent stakeholder feedback revealed the orientation cost was too high — people needed to know which mode they were in before engaging with individual metrics.

Executive Summary

Executive orientation — headline volume, savings rate, system health. Designed for speed, no drilling required.

Rebook Summary

Operational depth for teams tracking supplier performance and reshop efficiency over time.

Management

Proactively surfaced anomalies and metrics requiring action — not buried in a full data scan.

Reflection

What held, what didn’t, and what’s still open

The issue wasn’t a lack of data — it was an absence of structure around it. Each team interpreted the numbers differently, and there was no shared frame of reference for what required attention.

Do differently

Push harder for a shared requirements document in week one. Input arrived in fragments — a structured alignment session early on would have compressed the timeline without losing the depth of conversations that followed.

Still open

Real impact, faster decisions, fewer ad-hoc requests, better supplier response will only be measurable post-launch. The design is ready. The signal is pending.

The hard call

Equal-weight KPI display was the stakeholder ask. It wasn’t the right solution. The position held — but documenting the rationale formally, earlier, would have shortened the review cycles.

What surprised

The three-tab structure came from stakeholder conversations, not from the data. The data surfaced what existed. The people revealed what needed to be findable — and in what order.

Stand behind

Numbers over charts for low-margin comparisons. Three-tab separation of summary, insight, and action. Both were debated, both held, and both remain the right call for this product and audience.
