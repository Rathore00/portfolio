The Challenge
What changed
"Sometimes you've got to back out."
The original brief: iterate on a performing IBS care plan page. The new brief: transform it to promote two recipe ebooks, add a purchase-like experience, and capture leads — without breaking what was working.
Design Constraints
Existing page was performing — couldn't rebuild from scratch
Two ebooks needed equal visual weight — no primary/secondary hierarchy
Lead gen and checkout feel had to coexist without friction
Client hinted at more ebooks — the solution needed to scale without redesign
Approach: Product Thinking
Leverage existing assets — strong visuals, clear CTAs, user-friendly layout
Avoid prolonged research for a niche pivot — use prior audience understanding
Design for scalability: modular card layout that accepts future ebooks as content drops
Proposed an alternative (dedicated ebook hub) before executing client's direction
"Adapt, don't rebuild."
Design principle · This project
User Audience
Health-conscious IBS care plan users
Recipe-seeking, solution-oriented audience
New visitors via SEO pages
The Solution
What we built
01
Modular ebook card section
Each ebook as a distinct card — cover image, short descriptor, individual CTA. Future books plug in as a content operation, not a design one. "More coming soon" placeholder manages expectation.
02
Checkout-style lead capture
"Claim Now" triggered a single inline form (name, email, book preference). Threaded lead gen into the purchase flow — users felt they were completing a transaction, not filling a marketing form.
03
Visual cohesion with existing brand
Maintained the DailyBloom aesthetic — clean typography, calming health-focused palette. The ebook section felt like a natural extension, not a bolt-on.
Outcomes
Checkout-style section drove curiosity and increased lead capture
Scalable architecture reduced future redesign costs significantly
Delivered on time despite mid-project scope change
What I'd do differently
Push harder for the dedicated ebook hub — the three-goal tension on one page is a real UX compromise
A "decision log" mid-project prevented revisiting settled calls — I'd use this on every project now
