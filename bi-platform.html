Pain points

Where it hurt for the people using it

Three problems that showed up in the data, the screens and the room.

One shade of blue

Contrast fell short for users over 50, and the whole product felt like a retro artifact.

Good?Bad?

No way to tell good from bad

Across 30+ data visualizations, positive and negative areas looked identical.

FieldOperatorValue
FieldOperatorValue
AND / OR logic hidden in a menu

Filters cost too many clicks

A newly integrated project, mostly users over 50, exposed friction in the AND/OR filter in the reporting hub.

The business does not prioritize easy user journeys, only outcomes. So usability alone would never win the argument. The design had to prove credibility and capability too.

Feedback to insights

Feedback is a symptom. I look for the cause.

Three pieces of feedback, what I read in them, and what I did.

Heard

"Make it lively. Add color."

Insight

Color was missing meaning, not decoration. Contrast and positive versus negative were the real gaps.

Decision

I pitched a new palette on purpose, even though it sat outside the design system.

Observed

Users over 50 struggled with AND/OR filters.

Insight

The filters were hard to spot and took too many clicks to operate.

Decision

I cut the clicks and made the filters easier to see.

Heard

"The review points in the delete modal are wrong."

Insight

A warning that always looks the same teaches people to ignore it. It should reflect the real impact.

Decision

I made the impact messaging dynamic, with a green no-impact state.

How I worked

A small team and three honest iterations

The constraints I worked inside, and how the design moved.

System

A B2C design system only. I built a mini Enterprise Layer in DS for internal tools.

Outcome

Live, with work still ahead

Where the redesign stands today.

Live

The reporting hub and almost all major phases are in use. Third iteration approved.

Rolling out

The platform handles a lot of data. Migration takes time and some bugs still surface.

Next

The new design sits outside the design system. Formal adoption is the next step.

What I took from it

Change what you must and keep the rest familiar. Prove value through credibility and capability, then usability follows.

Results

People can read the data, find the filters and trust the warnings. And I am still finding more to fix.
